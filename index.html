Why Hightouch

I've sold the other side of this argument. That's why I believe it.

For a decade I sold packaged marketing and data clouds to enterprises that had already invested heavily in a data warehouse. The hardest part of every deal was the same question: why copy our customer data into another system? Hightouch's answer is the right one, and enterprise buyers are arriving at it on their own.

Where the data lives

The warehouse already won

Enterprises have spent years consolidating customer data in Snowflake, Databricks and BigQuery. A CDP that runs on that foundation, instead of beside it, removes the copy, the second identity graph and the reconciliation that slow every packaged-CDP rollout I've seen.

What the buyer funds

Outcomes, not a platform

CMOs fund revenue and retention, CIOs fund governance and simplicity, and CFOs fund time to value. Composable lets one deal answer all three, and AI Decisioning turns the conversation from audiences to measurable lift.

Why now

Agents need trusted context

Marketing agents are only as good as the customer data and brand context they can reach. Hightouch puts agents directly on governed warehouse data, which is the architecture buyers are moving toward as AI moves from pilots into production.

My edge in a Hightouch deal: I know how the incumbent is sold, how it's bought, and where it breaks after signature, because I was in the room for all three.

Canada: the next market

Canada is its own buying room. Whoever opens it should already be in it.

This role covers US accounts, and that is where my focus goes first. Hightouch already hires in Toronto and Montreal and counts Canadian brands like Aritzia among its customers. When Canada becomes a market of its own, here is how I would read it.

1 · Concentration

A few hundred enterprises, and everyone talks

Canada's enterprise market is concentrated in national retailers, banks and insurers, telcos, QSR brands and sports and entertainment groups. Marketing and data leaders move between them and compare notes. One strong Canadian reference does more work here than ten US logos, so the first three wins should be chosen for reference value, not just deal size.

2 · Privacy and residency

Governance is the opening line, not an objection

Quebec's Law 25 phased in between 2022 and 2024 and puts privacy impact assessments in front of new information systems. Federally regulated banks and insurers carry their own data-governance expectations. A CDP that keeps customer data inside the customer's own governed warehouse, often in a Canadian cloud region, answers the CIO's first question before it's asked.

3 · Loyalty-rich data

Built for decisioning

Canadian consumers live inside loyalty programs, from grocery and pharmacy to fuel, travel and banking. That means first-party data is deep but often trapped in programs that were designed for points, not personalization. AI Decisioning is a natural next step for those teams, and it's a story I've told in loyalty-heavy retail for years.

4 · Two languages

Quebec needs its own coverage

I've closed in Quebec, including one of the first CDP deals in Canada at Harnois Énergies and a net-new CDP win with Groupe CH across the Montréal Canadiens, Laval Rocket and Montreal Jazz Festival. I'm not fluent in French, so serious Quebec coverage means a French-speaking AE and SE. That hire should be part of the Canada plan from the start.
